# Decision: market-only margin and total forecasts

Retain production v1.16. Market-only target forecasts are a more promising source of additional context than the earlier box-score target models, but the tested rolling residuals do not establish added value. Preserve the passing-by-predicted-total arm as a research challenger; this test does not qualify a production replacement.

Across 812 common flat 1u moneyline bets from exposed 2023–25 walk-forward seasons, production returned +29.06u, ROI +3.58% ±2.52 percentage points SE. Passing-by-predicted-total returned +46.32u, +5.70% ±2.54. Its paired ROI gain was +2.13 pp, 98.75% week-cluster interval [-0.27,+4.89]. Returns improved in each evaluated season, but superiority remains unresolved. The same-row market favorite returned +0.18u, +0.02% ±2.47; the market-correct null was -4.10%. Lower binary win log loss is better: the challenger scored .606426 versus production .605605 on 815 binary outcomes. It improved win log loss only in 2024. Brier was also worse. ROI was reported rather than used for fitting or recipe selection.

Adding both forecast features without their interactions returned +44.50u / +5.48%, LL .606827. Passing-by-total adds only +0.22 pp to this additive model, with a descriptive interval touching zero; its LL gain .000402 has an interval crossing zero. Margin-by-total returned +35.31u / +4.35%, LL .607230, worse than the additive model on both scores. Replacing learned total with the market total returned +39.44u / +4.86%, LL .606498. Predicted-total source gain +0.85 pp has a 95% interval touching zero, and LL gain .000072 [-.000723,+.000875] is unresolved. These results cannot attribute the entire challenger return improvement to either the learned total or the interaction.

The annually selected win recipe, chosen using only earlier-season log loss, returned +31.93u / +3.93%, LL .608215. Its ROI gain +0.35 pp [-1.17,+1.94] versus production is unresolved and its proper scores declined. The matched-history production comparator scored LL .607431; the challenger improves that score, but full-history production is the deployment comparator. Shortening the training cohort does not by itself create an improvement.

On 816 target-scored games, market relationships reduced margin NLL from 3.957840 to 3.956549 and total NLL from 3.981466 to 3.978169. Margin RMSE changed 12.653 → 12.638 points; total 12.959 → 12.916. Gains are small and unresolved. Every fixed residual rule scored worse NLL than market relationships alone for both targets. Earlier-season selection always chose relationships alone for margin; totals chose half-life 16 / prior 4 in 2023–24, then relationships alone in 2025–26. The selected total NLL is 3.979189, worse than relationships alone by .001021, interval [-.002966,+.000818] for improvement. This suggests recent team over/underperformance mostly adds noise under the tested shrinkage and decay rules; it does not prove all residual models lack value.

At actual two-sided no-vig market prices and with pushes excluded identically, selected margin cover LL was .693529 versus market .693588; selected total over LL .690784 versus .693319. Both improvement intervals cross zero. Direct selected-margin winner probabilities returned +4.08u / +0.50%, LL .607748 versus market .608072. That modest probability change did not approach production returns. A Gaussian centered on a spread/total is an approximate distribution benchmark, not the bookmaker's full implied score distribution.

For archived SF at SEA, the market-only model predicts Seattle by 3.229 points versus market 3.0, and total 46.235 versus 45.5. The passing-total production stack remains 94.07% Seattle, compared with archived production 93.90%. Market-only modeling removes the extreme target margin produced by the earlier shared-box-feature model, but does not repair production's extreme win probability. All current outputs are counterfactuals; schedule total/cover/over capture times are unknown.

All residual histories, stage-one coefficients, dispersion estimates, stage-two fits and annual selection labels precede the evaluated week/season. Histories are team perspectives with decay, offseason retention, pseudo-game shrinkage and audited effective support. Stage one uses only market inputs and earlier outcomes; production box features enter only the explicitly labeled stage-two interaction tests. Gaussian NFL key-number/support limitations, historical design exposure and quote timing prevent native forward qualification; bootstrap intervals omit research-design selection uncertainty.

Recommended next test: if the owner wants a forward challenger, freeze the existing passing-total specification alongside production at native pregame locks, with contemporary total prices and no retrospective retuning. Keep rolling residuals out of that candidate unless a new predeclared test establishes their incremental value. No new forward experiment, production formula or ledger edit was made here.
